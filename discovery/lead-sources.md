# Where to find both segments

Likelihood is a guess until the log says otherwise.

## Plan A, sold now: operations owners (35 of the 50 names)

Organizations whose AI agents take consequential financial or operational actions.

1. **Support automation with refund or credit authority.** Companies using Sierra, Decagon, Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI agents or Salesforce Agentforce at e-commerce, subscription SaaS, marketplaces, travel and fintech. Titles: Head of Support Operations, Refunds, Customer Operations, Trust and Safety.
2. **Fintech operations.** Disputes, chargebacks, limit changes, account modifications. Titles: Payments Operations, Risk Operations, Fraud Operations.
3. **Procurement and accounts payable automation.** Coupa and SAP Ariba customers with AI copilots or AP agents. Titles: Procurement Operations, AP Manager, Controller.
4. **Internal IT and access provisioning.** ServiceNow and Okta workflows with AI actions. Titles: IT Operations, Identity, Access Governance.

## Plan B, counted now: platform and developer-tooling leads (15 of the 50 names)

Teams whose coding agents or AI applications caused a consequential failure: deleted or corrupted data, a broken invariant, a bad migration, an unsafe side effect, a bypassed check.

1. **Teams using coding agents at scale.** Public talks, engineering blog posts and job posts that mention agent-written pull requests. Titles: Head of Platform, Developer Productivity, Developer Experience, Engineering Enablement.
2. **Published agent postmortems.** Authors of write-ups where an agent or an AI-written change broke production.
3. **Agent-framework communities** such as LangChain and the OpenAI Agents SDK: people asking how to stop an agent repeating a failure.
4. **Recorder and policy tool users.** People filing issues on agent recorders and on OPA already care about this problem.

## Warm paths

Ask every mentor and former colleague for two introductions: one person who owns refunds, payments or procurement operations, and one platform lead at a team using coding agents.

## Qualify when

- the agent takes an action with a financial or operational effect;
- a loss owner or platform owner is identifiable;
- there is public evidence of the deployment.

## Disqualify when

- chat-only agents with no execution;
- no identifiable owner;
- the company is too small to have an owner separate from the founder;
- the problem is content quality, hallucination scoring or model evaluation;
- the only incidents are from traditional automation (record them, do not target them);
- individuals using personal assistants (not built: the assistant vendor owns the enforcement surface).
